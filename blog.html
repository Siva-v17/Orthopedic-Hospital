Myth vs fact

Common bone and joint myths

Myth

Cracking knuckles causes arthritis

Fact: Studies have not linked knuckle cracking to arthritis. Persistent pain or swelling is still worth checking.

Myth

Joint pain is a normal part of ageing

Fact: Age raises the risk, but ongoing pain has causes that can usually be treated.

Myth

Rest is the best cure for back pain

Fact: Prolonged bed rest can slow recovery. Gentle movement is usually advised.

Myth

Joint replacement lasts only a few years

Fact: Modern implants often last many years, and results depend on materials, surgery and care.

Myth

Running ruins your knees

Fact: Sensible running with good form and footwear is not known to cause knee damage on its own.

Myth

Only older women get osteoporosis

Fact: Men and younger adults can also lose bone density, especially with low vitamin D or inactivity.

Know the warning signs

When to see an orthopedic doctor

Mild aches often settle with rest. These signs mean it is time to get checked rather than wait.

Book a Consultation

See a specialist if you notice

  • Pain that lasts more than two weeks
  • Swelling, redness or warmth around a joint
  • Difficulty putting weight on a leg
  • Numbness or tingling in arms or legs
  • A joint that locks, clicks painfully or gives way
  • Pain that wakes you at night

Sudden severe pain, a visible deformity or an injury after a fall needs emergency care.
